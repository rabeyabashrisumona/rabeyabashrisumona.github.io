RDXNet — Hybrid Brain Tumor Classification

A hybrid model that fuses ResNet50, DenseNet121 and Xception features to classify brain MRI scans into glioma, meningioma, pituitary tumor or no tumor. Published in Healthcare Analytics (Elsevier, 2025).

Overview

Problem
Diagnosing brain tumors from MRI is time-consuming, expensive and depends heavily on radiologists' expertise. Tumors also vary widely in appearance, which makes automated multiclass classification difficult.
Approach
Evaluate ResNet50, DenseNet121 and Xception individually with transfer learning, then combine them into one hybrid model, RDXNet, through feature fusion. Check robustness with K-fold cross-validation and interpretability with Grad-CAM.
My role and contribution
First author. Model design, preprocessing and training pipeline, and evaluation.
Technical stack
  • Python
  • Transfer learning
  • ResNet50
  • DenseNet121
  • Xception
  • Feature fusion
  • Grad-CAM
  • K-fold cross-validation

Data

7,023 MRI images from three public datasets (Br35H :: Brain Tumor Detection 2020, the Figshare Brain Tumor Dataset and Radiopaedia MRI scans), labelled in four classes: glioma, meningioma, pituitary tumor and no tumor.

Method

Images are cleaned and preprocessed (corrupted or incomplete scans removed, skull stripping, grayscale conversion, resizing, intensity normalisation, label and one-hot encoding, augmentation). Each pretrained backbone is first trained and evaluated on its own; their features are then fused in RDXNet with a custom classification head.

Evaluation

The individual backbones and RDXNet are compared on the same split, and K-fold cross-validation across multiple data splits checks that the result is not an artefact of one partition. Per-class performance is reported with a confusion matrix and ROC curves.

ROC analysis

Explainability

Grad-CAM heatmaps show which image regions drive each prediction, so a reader can check whether the model attends to the tumor rather than to unrelated structures.

Limitations

  • Trained and tested on public datasets only; performance on data from other hospitals was not evaluated in this study.
  • Glioma and meningioma are the most often confused classes.
  • Research software. Not clinically validated and not intended for diagnostic use.
